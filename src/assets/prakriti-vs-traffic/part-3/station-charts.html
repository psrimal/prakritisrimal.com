Week 3 on the Road

Drive times between Tilak Nagar and Whitefield, sampled every ten minutes through a second full working week, this time with the metro stations picked fresh each evening.

Prakriti Srimal   ·  

Every ten minutes, in a morning window (07:00–11:30) and an evening window (17:00–20:30), a script asked the Google Routes API how long the drive between GNR Kalyan Mantapa and Earth Centre in Whitefield would take right now, and how long it would take with no traffic at all. The gap between the two is the delay.

The route was also cut into nine pieces, and the feeder legs to the metro stations were timed as well. Each evening a station search chose which stations to time for the next day, and the route was recomputed too. Nothing below is an image: each figure is drawn live, and the slider underneath it changes what is being looked at. Hover any mark for its numbers. Morning is the outbound trip to Whitefield; evening is the return.

Reliability

An average hides the bad days. What matters for planning is how long a trip can take on the unlucky one in twenty. Two views of that follow. The first shows the 5th percentile, the average and the 95th percentile of the congestion ratio, so the band is the spread between a lucky run and an unlucky one. The second is the Buffer Time Index: the 95th-percentile travel time minus the average, divided by the average. A value of 0.30 means leaving 30% earlier than the average trip needs, on top of it, to arrive on time 19 days out of 20.

Each half-hour has only about fifteen readings behind it (three ticks over five days), so treat the percentiles as indicative, not exact. Half-hours with fewer than eight readings are left blank.

The Metro Feeders

Getting to a metro station is a drive of its own. These are road-only times, so they bound the feeder part of a multimodal trip but say nothing about the ride itself.

Which station is quickest to reach?

Stations are averaged over the days they were actually timed, so one that was only chosen on some days rests on fewer readings.

Which stations were chosen each day?

The station search picks up to eight entry stations and eight exit stations per direction every evening, for the next day. A station that appears all five days is a stable choice; one that appears once was marginal. Entry is where the trip leaves the car for the metro; exit is where it picks the car back up.

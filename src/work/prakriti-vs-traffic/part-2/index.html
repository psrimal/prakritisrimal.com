Prakriti vs Traffic: Part 2

How long would it actually take me to get to work by metro?

Prakriti Srimal   ·   29th October, 2026.

Google Maps says 1 hr 34. But that isn't the time from my front door to my desk.

In Part 1, I put what Maps promised next to what a stopwatch said. The metro barely moved, from 1 hr 29 to 1 hr 34. But one number per route hides everything I actually fiddle with before leaving: how I get to the station, how long I wait for a train, and how I get from the last station to my desk.

The metro isn't the journey. It's the middle of the journey.

So I turned the routes into a race. Five stations I could start from, six I could finish at, and every combination run from my door to the office, for any time I might leave.

The Method

Every route is a chain of smaller journeys, from door to desk. I broke it down into five stages:

  • Get there The road travel time from my door to a starting station, based on the minute I leave. I considered five options: Banashankari, Jayanagar, JP Nagar, RV Road and South End Circle.
  • Gates A flat 3 minutes from the road to the platform: gates, stairs and the walk down.
  • Wait However long until the next train actually leaves, based on the metro timetable. This is why two routes can finish in the very same minute: they catch the same train.
  • Ride The metro journey itself, including any change of line, with platform transfer times taken from the timetable.
  • Out A flat 2 minutes to exit the station, followed by the road journey to the office. I considered six finishing stations at the Whitefield end, including Sri Sathya Sai Hospital, the closest to my desk.

That gives me 5 starting stations × 6 finishing stations = 30 possible routes for every departure time.

I ran the race across one working week, with two departure windows: mornings from 7:00 to 11:30 AM and evenings from 5:00 to 8:30 PM. For every departure time, all 30 routes compete to see which gets me to the office first.

The Experiment

Now, let's see them race.

Pick morning or evening, a day (or the Mon–Fri typical), and a departure time. Each dot represents a route travelling to a station, waiting for a train, riding the metro, and completing the last stretch to the office. The first ones in light up.

The animation is sped up (400×), so it doesn't run at real-world speed. The times on the board are the calculated journey times.

Findings

I ran this half expecting my usual station to come out on top. It didn't.

In the mornings, Jayanagar to Sri Sathya Sai Hospital came first in 83 of the 140 departure slots I tried, with South End Circle a distant second at 39.

The quickest door-to-desk journeys landed between 68 and 90 minutes in the morning, and 76 and 90 minutes in the evening, regardless of when I left.

So, the station I usually head towards wasn't necessarily a bad choice. But there were other combinations worth considering, and the quickest route wasn't always the most obvious one.

Limitations

  • Old timetable The metro schedule is an unofficial BMRCL GTFS feed dated 17 August 2026. I downloaded it on 3 October and never refreshed it. If BMRCL has changed timings since, the race doesn't know.
  • Modelled stops Nearly every station-to-station time in that feed is interpolated, so the metro journey times are modelled rather than observed.
  • Data is static Each journey, including the time to the office, is calculated once in advance for its departure time and then replayed. Nothing updates if a train is late or the road changes while you're on it.
  • Auto estimates The first and last stretches use Google's traffic-aware driving times as a stand-in for an auto. There is no separate walking leg in the calculation.

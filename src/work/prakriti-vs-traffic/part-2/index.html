Prakriti vs Traffic: Part 2

How long would it actually take me to get to work by metro?

Prakriti Srimal   ·   29th October, 2026.

Google Maps says 1 hr 34. But that isn’t the time from my front door to my desk.

In Part 1 I put what Maps promised next to what a stopwatch said. The metro barely moved, from 1 hr 29 to 1 hr 34, which is why I like it. But one number per route hides everything I actually fiddle with before leaving: how I get to the station, how long I wait for a train, and how I get from the last station to my desk.

The metro isn’t the journey. It’s the middle of the journey.

So I turned the routes into a race. Five stations I could start from, six I could finish at, and every combination run from my door to the office, for any time I might leave.

The race

Pick morning or evening, a day (or the Mon–Fri typical) and a departure time. Each dot is one route. It travels to a station, waits, rides, and finishes the last stretch. The first ones in light up.

The animation is sped up (400×), so it does not run at real-world speed. The times on the board are the calculated ones.

What I counted

Every route is the same chain, door to desk:

  • Get there The road time from my door to a starting station, for the minute I leave. Five options: Banashankari, Jayanagar, JP Nagar, RV Road and South End Circle.
  • Gates A flat 3 minutes from the road to the platform: gates, stairs, the walk down.
  • Wait However long until the next train actually leaves, from the metro timetable. This is why two stations can finish in the very same minute: they catch the same train.
  • Ride The ride itself, including any change of line, with the platform transfer times taken from the timetable.
  • Out A flat 2 minutes to get out of the station, then the road time to the office. Six options at the Whitefield end, including Sri Sathya Sai Hospital, the closest to my desk.

That is 5 starts × 6 finishes = 30 routes for every departure time. The race covers one working week, Monday 12 to Friday 16 October, across a morning window (7:00 to 11:30 AM) and an evening one (5:00 to 8:30 PM), and simply asks which of the 30 gets in first.

The stations

Before the race ranks anything, this is the piece it turns on: how long it takes to reach each starting station, and to get from each finishing station to the office. The quickest station to reach is not always the one that gets me in first.

Same week as the race, 12 to 16 October.

What it showed

I ran this half expecting my usual station to come out on topp, it didn’t. In the mornings, Jayanagar to Sri Sathya Sai Hospital came first in 83 of the 140 departure slots I tried, with South End Circle a distant second at 39.

The metro showed expected results: door to desk, the best route landed somewhere between 68 and 90 minutes in the morning and 76 and 90 in the evening, more or less regardless of when I left.

Limitations

  • Old timetable The metro schedule is an unofficial BMRCL GTFS feed dated 17 August 2026. I downloaded it on 3 October and never refreshed it. If BMRCL has changed timings since, the race doesn’t know.
  • Modelled stops Nearly every station-to-station time in that feed is interpolated and the metro times are modelled.
  • Worked out up front Each journey, including the time to the office, is calculated once, in advance, for its departure time and then replayed. Nothing updates if a train is late or the road changes while you’re on it.
  • Auto The first and last stretches use Google’s traffic-aware driving times as a stand-in for an auto. There is no walking leg in the calculation.
  • Metro data: unofficial BMRCL GTFS by Vonter, ODbL-1.0. Feed dated 17 August 2026.
  • Metro routing is an earliest-arrival search over the timetable, with transfer times from the feed.
